Do (a) and (b) completely. On (c), here is the correction, and you will

WIN RATE ON ITS OWN IS THE EASIEST NUMBER IN TRADING TO FAKE.
Any strategy can show 80% or 90% wins by taking tiny profits and letting
losers run. Nine wins of $100 and one loss of $1,000 is a 90% win rate and
a losing system. The number that says whether a system makes money is
EXPECTANCY — (win% × average win) − (loss% × average loss) — together
with PROFIT FACTOR, MAX DRAWDOWN and how many trades the numbers rest on.
A 30% win rate can be excellent and a 70% one a disaster; only the pair
tells you which. Odin already knows this; it is written into his brief.

So the target is NOT "80% win rate at any cost". The target is:

  A SCORECARD (section 6) THAT ODIN MUST BEAT, IN WHICH WIN RATE IS ONE
  OF SEVEN NUMBERS, AND ONE LANE OF STRATEGIES — DEFINED-RISK SHORT
  PREMIUM (section 8) — WHERE 75–85% WIN RATES ARE NORMAL AND HONEST
  BECAUSE THE LOSSES ARE CAPPED BY CONSTRUCTION, NOT BY HOPE.

That lane is the only place an 80% figure means anything. You will build
it, measure it properly, and Odin will report it next to expectancy every
single time so the number can never lie to me.

And on "two to three weeks": you will build, backtest, walk-forward and
put on paper everything in this brief inside 21 days (section 17). What
you will NOT do is claim a strategy is proven in 21 days of paper. The

6.  THE SCORECARD — THE SEVEN NUMBERS, AND THE GATES
=====================================================================

6.1  Per trader, per strategy, and for the whole council, always:
     1. Trades          closed trades in the window (the denominator)
     2. Win rate        wins ÷ trades
     3. Avg win / Avg loss  in $ and as a ratio (payoff ratio)
     4. Expectancy      (win% × avg win) − (loss% × avg loss), in $ per
                        trade and in R (multiples of initial risk)
     5. Profit factor   gross wins ÷ gross losses
     6. Max drawdown    (peak − trough) ÷ peak, on the equity curve
     7. Sharpe (daily)  mean daily return ÷ stdev, × √252, and Sortino
     plus: exposure (% of days in a position), average hold time,
     largest single loss, current losing streak, longest streak ever,
     realized vs unrealized (always separate), fees paid.

6.2  Every number carries its denominator. "Win rate 81% (n=13)" is the
     only allowed way to print a win rate. A number with n under 30 is
     printed in a dimmer style on the panel and prefixed "early:" in
     text. Odin says "thirteen trades so far" out loud.

6.3  What "beating the scorecard" means, per strategy, computed on the
     out-of-sample window and on live paper, not on the fit window:
     * expectancy > 0 after slippage and fees
     * profit factor ≥ 1.3
     * max drawdown ≤ 8% of the trader's $10,000
     * Sharpe ≥ 1.0 over the window
     * at least 30 closed trades in the window
     * the WORST walk-forward fold is still positive expectancy
     * win rate: reported, compared to the strategy's own EXPECTED band
       (section 7) — a strategy whose win rate is far above its band is
       flagged "suspicious", not celebrated, and the Forge checks for
       look-ahead or a broken stop before anyone smiles.

6.4  Graduation gates (documentation only; nothing enables real money):
     G0 CANDIDATE   written down with exact rules
     G1 BACKTESTED  passes 6.3 on 5+ years in-sample
     G2 WALKED      passes 6.3 on every out-of-sample fold (12.3)
     G3 STRESSED    survives Monte Carlo (12.4) and parameter jitter (12.5)
     G4 SHADOW      ran 10 trading days on paper with no order size
                    (signals only, logged), matching the backtest's
                    signal timing within one bar
     G5 PAPER       trading paper money, ≥ 30 closed trades, passes 6.3
     G6 SEASONED    ≥ 90 trading days on paper, ≤ 8% drawdown, passes 6.3
     Odin's report shows each strategy's gate. The readiness tool lists
     G6 as one of its (unmet, documentation-only) conditions.

6.5  The council-level scorecard adds: correlation between traders'
     daily P&L (a council of five that all lose on the same day is one
     trader wearing five hats), portfolio heat (10.3), and "cash drag"
     (how much of the $50,000 total sat idle).

=====================================================================
8.  THE HIGH-WIN-RATE LANE — DEFINED-RISK SHORT PREMIUM, DONE HONESTLY
=====================================================================

This is the only place an "80%" is a real number. Selling a put credit
spread 15–20 delta on SPY, 30–45 days out, wins about 75–85% of the time
over long histories because the market only has to NOT fall a lot. The
catch is the shape: many small wins, occasional larger losses. Two things
make it honest instead of a trap:
  (1) the loss is CAPPED by the long leg — the worst case is known before
      entry and it is what the risk engine sizes against;
  (2) the report shows expectancy and profit factor next to the win rate
      every time, so a run of wins never hides the tail.

8.1  Instruments: SPY, QQQ, IWM options, Alpaca paper, multi-leg orders.
     Nothing else. No single-leg short options, ever (undefined risk).
     No naked calls, ever. No 0DTE, ever.

8.2  G1 — put credit spread
     * Entry: regime engine says "normal" or "calm" (section 9); IV rank
       of the underlying ≥ 20 (from a 252-day window of ATM IV the Forge
       maintains); no earnings/FOMC inside the next 7 days (E-calendar).
     * Structure: sell the put nearest 15–20 delta, buy the put $3–$5
       lower (P: width 3–5), 30–45 DTE (P).
     * Credit: at least 25% of the width (a $5 wide spread must bring in
       ≥ $1.25) or no trade.
     * Size: max loss = width − credit, per contract ×100. The risk engine
       sizes so that max loss ≤ the per-trade risk budget (10.1), and
       total open defined-risk ≤ 30% of that trader's equity.
     * Management: take profit at 50% of credit; stop at 2× credit lost
       (buy back when the spread trades at 2× what we sold it for);
       time exit at 21 DTE regardless.
     * Expected: win rate 72–82%, payoff ratio ≈ 0.35–0.5, profit factor
       1.2–1.6 after fees. If the Forge shows > 88% win rate, it is
       broken; find the bug.
  G2 — iron condor: G1 plus the mirror call spread at 15–20 delta, same
     DTE, only when regime = "calm" and the 20-day realized vol is below
     the 252-day median. Manage each side separately at 50% / 2×.
  G3/G4 — same as G1/G2 on QQQ and IWM. One underlying per trader.
  G5 — the wheel, paper only, on SPY in whole 100-share lots the sim
     permits with $10,000 — it does not (SPY ≈ $600). SKIP G5 on SPY;
     run it on a lower-priced liquid ETF only if the Forge finds one that
     passes; otherwise mark "not feasible at this account size" and move
     on. Do not force it.

8.3  Options data: Alpaca provides options chains and quotes for paper.
     Cache the chain once per tick per underlying, never per contract.
     Compute delta yourself (Black-Scholes with the chain's IV) if the
     feed does not supply it; document which.

8.4  Fees: $0.65 per contract per leg each way in the sim; whatever
     Alpaca paper reports, in the Hall. Report fees separately.

8.5  Assignment risk: in paper, early assignment is simulated rarely;
     handle the case (a short put assigned → you now own 100 shares →
     close them next tick and log ASSIGNED). Never let an assignment
     leave a position the Ledger does not know about (5.4 catches it).

8.6  The whole lane runs on ONE councillor (FRIGGA, id "freya", section
     13) so its win rate is never blended into the trend traders' and

10. THE RISK ENGINE — THE PART THAT LETS THE ANIMAL BE AGGRESSIVE
=====================================================================

10.1 Per trade: risk 1% of the trader's equity ($100 on $10,000) from
     entry to stop; for defined-risk spreads, max loss = the risk. Size
     = risk ÷ (entry − stop) rounded DOWN to whole shares/contracts;
     if that is zero, no trade.
10.2 Volatility scaling: for A/C families, stop = 2× ATR(14) (P: 1.5–3);
     for B, 3× ATR or the structural level, whichever is closer.
10.3 Portfolio heat: sum of open risk across the council ≤ 4% of total
     equity ($2,000 of $50,000). New entries blocked above that.
10.4 Day-trade guard: equities/ETFs — a position opened today is not
     closed today except by its stop; the PDT counter is tracked and a
     4th day trade in 5 days is refused with a log line. Crypto and
     options have their own rules (options: never open and close the
     same spread the same day except at the stop).
10.5 Daily loss limit per trader: −2% of equity closes all of that
     trader's positions and halts it until the next session. Council
     daily loss limit −3% halts the council. Weekly −5% halts until
     Monday and Odin says so in Telegram.
10.6 Drawdown governor: at 5% drawdown from peak, every size halves. At
     8%, the trader stops opening positions until it has been reviewed
     by the Forge and re-promoted (the report shows "in review").
10.7 Correlation guard: no two traders long the same direction in SPY
     and QQQ at the same time unless the combined risk fits 10.3 and the
     regime is "uptrend + calm/normal". GLD/SLV, SPY/IWM same rule.
10.8 Streak guard: after 5 consecutive losses in one strategy, its size
     halves for the next 5 trades; after 8, it goes to shadow (signals
     only) and the Forge re-runs it.
10.9 Fat-finger guard: no order larger than 25% of the trader's equity
     in notional; no options order with more than 5 contracts per leg;
     no order at a price more than 2% away from the last quote.
10.10 Every refusal is logged with the rule number. Odin can be asked
     "why didn't Fandral trade today" and answer from the log.


=====================================================================
11. EXECUTION — WHAT HAPPENS ON A TICK, EXACTLY
=====================================================================

Every 5 minutes during the session (crypto: every 15 minutes, all week):
  1. If the market is closed for that trader's instrument → return. One
     read, zero writes.
  2. Read regime:today (one read). If "storm" and the family is off →
     manage existing positions only.
  3. Read the trader's portfolio (one read). Fetch quotes for its open
     symbol(s) in one batched call. Update unrealized in memory.
  4. Check stops / targets / time exits / assignment → if any fires,
     place the closing order, wait for the fill status (one read),
     write the portfolio ONCE, append one trade line.
  5. If flat and the strategy's signal fires on the completed bar (never
     the forming bar) and every risk rule passes → place the order with
     a clientOrderId, wait for status, write ONCE. If partial → keep the
     remainder open for at most 2 ticks, then cancel the remainder and
     size the stop to the filled quantity.
  6. If nothing changed → write nothing. Not the equity, not a timestamp.
  7. At the close: one equity sample per trader, one journal line per
     trader (13.4), one report marker.
  8. Any exception → catch, log with the trader id and rule, never let
     one trader's error stop another's tick, never write a half-state.

12. THE FORGE — WHERE ODIN GETS SMART
=====================================================================

C:\Asgard\forge\ — Python 3.12 venv, wheels only. Scheduled task at 02:00
Pacific nightly and `forge run` on demand. Logs to C:\Asgard\logs\forge\.

12.1 Data layer: pull and cache to Parquet (section 4.8). One loader
     function per source. Every bar row carries its source and fetch
     time. A "gaps" report lists any missing sessions per symbol.

12.2 Backtest engine: use backtesting.py for single-asset rule strategies
     (simple, actively maintained) and PyBroker for anything that needs
     walk-forward or ML features (walk-forward is pre-wired there).
     vectorbt's open-source version is in maintenance mode (development
     moved to the paid PRO tier) — use it only for fast parameter sweeps
     if PyBroker is too slow, and pin the version. Do not use backtrader
     (frozen). NautilusTrader is more than we need; note it under Later.
     The same fill/slippage/fee model as section 5.5 is used here —
     implement it ONCE in forge/fills.py and have every engine call it.

12.3 Walk-forward: for each strategy, anchored expanding-window folds —
     fit on 3 years, test on the next 6 months, roll forward 6 months,
     across the whole history. Parameters are chosen on the fit window
     ONLY by expectancy-per-trade with a drawdown penalty, never by win
     rate. Report each fold's out-of-sample scorecard. The strategy's
     "walked" verdict is the WORST fold, not the average.

12.4 Monte Carlo: shuffle the out-of-sample trade sequence 2,000 times
     and skip 10% of trades at random each run; report the 5th-percentile
     drawdown and the probability of a 10% drawdown. A strategy whose
     5th-percentile drawdown exceeds 12% is not promoted.

12.5 Parameter stability: for the chosen parameters, jitter each ±20%
     and re-run; if the expectancy sign flips or profit factor falls
     below 1.1 in more than a third of the jittered runs, the parameters
     are a lucky point on a cliff — do not promote; widen the search.

12.6 Look-ahead and leakage checks, automated: every indicator is
     computed with data up to the PREVIOUS completed bar; a unit test
     shifts every input by one bar and asserts the signals shift with
     it. Any strategy whose backtest win rate exceeds its expected band
     by more than 15 points is quarantined until the test passes.

12.7 The tournament and promotion file. Every night:
     * re-run every strategy in the catalogue on data through yesterday
     * produce forge/out/verdicts.json: per strategy — gate reached (G0–
       G3 from the Forge; G4–G6 come from the Hall's live record), the
       scorecard on the last out-of-sample fold, the chosen parameters,
       the regime families it is allowed in, and a one-line plain-English
       verdict ("passes; weakest fold PF 1.22; promote to FANDRAL lane")
     * sign the file (HMAC with a secret only the Forge and the Hall
       hold; wrangler secret put FORGE_HMAC < file) and upload it to the
       Hall's POST /forge/verdicts. The Hall verifies the signature and
       stores it as ONE KV key.
     * The Hall assigns strategies to lanes ONLY from that file, and only
       at the next session open, and only if the strategy's gate is ≥ G3.
       A strategy currently trading that loses its G3 in the nightly run
       is moved to shadow at the next open, positions managed to exit by
       their own rules. Odin reports every promotion and demotion.

12.8 Research that is NOT promoted this sprint but is built and run so
     Odin has real answers: E3, F2, C5, the SEC insider-buying overlay,
     a simple ML filter (gradient-boosted "will this B1 signal win?"
     classifier on 20 features, walk-forward, PyBroker) — reported as
     research, gate G1 at most, never traded until a later brief.

12.9 The Forge writes docs/RESEARCH.md nightly: a human-readable table of
     all forty, their gate, their weakest-fold numbers, and the sentence.
     That file is the thing I can read on my phone.

     journal makes every loss explainable, and (e) the tournament
     retires a strategy the moment a better one passes the same gates.
     That is intelligence you can audit. Say it that way in the README.

14. THE ANTI-OVERFIT CHECKLIST — RUN BEFORE ANYTHING IS PROMOTED
=====================================================================

  [ ] parameters chosen on fit windows only; out-of-sample never touched
  [ ] worst fold, not average, is the verdict
  [ ] fewer than 4 free parameters per strategy
  [ ] each parameter's range was set BEFORE the search and is in the spec
  [ ] ≥ 100 trades in the full-history backtest, ≥ 30 per fold
  [ ] fills at next open, slippage and fees applied, partials simulated
  [ ] one-bar shift test passes (12.6)
  [ ] Monte Carlo 5th-percentile drawdown ≤ 12%
  [ ] ±20% parameter jitter keeps expectancy positive in ≥ 2/3 of runs
  [ ] win rate inside the family's expected band (or explained)
  [ ] survivorship: ETF universe only, plus current large caps with a
      note that single names carry survivorship bias — no promotion of
      a single-name strategy this sprint
  [ ] the same strategy on a "cousin" instrument (SPY↔QQQ, GLD↔SLV)
      shows the same sign of expectancy
  [ ] a human sentence explains WHY the edge should exist (behavioural,
      structural, calendar). "It backtested well" is not a why.

25. GLOSSARY — SO I CAN READ WHAT ODIN SAYS (goes in docs/GLOSSARY.md)
=====================================================================

  Paper trading     Fake money, real prices. Every number Odin gives me
                    is from this. No real money exists in this system.
  Win rate          Out of every 100 closed trades, how many made money.
                    On its own it means almost nothing.
  Average win/loss  What a winning trade makes on average, and what a
                    losing one costs. The pair beside win rate is what
                    tells you if the system makes money.
  Expectancy        What one trade is worth on average, in dollars:
                    (win% × avg win) − (loss% × avg loss). Above zero,
                    the system makes money over time. Below zero, it
                    loses, no matter the win rate.
  R                 One "R" is the amount risked on a trade (our 1%).
                    "+2R" means the trade made twice what it risked.
  Profit factor     Total money won ÷ total money lost. 1.0 is break-
                    even. 1.3+ is respectable. 3+ is usually a bug.
  Drawdown          How far the account fell from its highest point,
                    in percent. 8% is our line; 5% halves the size.
  Sharpe            Return per unit of wobble. Above 1 is good for a
                    simple system. Above 3 is usually a bug.
  n                 How many trades the number is built on. "81% (n=13)"
                    means thirteen trades — not enough to trust yet.
  Regime            What kind of market it is right now: calm / normal
                    / elevated / storm, and uptrend / range / downtrend.
                    Every strategy has regimes it is not allowed in.
  Gate (G0–G6)      How far a strategy has been proven: written down →
                    backtested → walked forward → stress-tested → shadow
                    → paper trading → seasoned. Nothing is "proven" at
                    G3; G6 is ninety trading days.
  Backtest          Running the rules on old prices to see what would
                    have happened. Easy to cheat by accident; section 14
                    is the list of ways we refuse to.
  Walk-forward      Testing on data the rules never saw when their
                    settings were chosen, in rolling slices. Our verdict
                    is the WORST slice.
  Monte Carlo       Shuffling the order of trades thousands of times to
                    see how bad a run of luck could get.
  Slippage          The difference between the price you wanted and the
                    price you got. We charge it on every simulated fill.
  Stop              A price at which the trade is closed automatically
                    to cap the loss.
  ATR               Average true range — how much the price typically
                    moves in a day. Stops are set in ATRs.
  Delta             For an option, roughly the chance it ends up in the
                    money. A 15-delta put is about a 15% chance.
  DTE               Days to expiry. We sell 30–45 days out and exit by
                    21.
  IV rank           Whether options are expensive right now compared to
                    the last year. We sell premium when it is ≥ 20.
  Credit spread     Sell one option, buy a cheaper one further out. You
                    keep the difference if the market behaves; your
                    worst case is fixed the moment you enter.
  Iron condor       A credit spread on both sides at once. Wins when the
                    market goes nowhere.
  Lane              One councillor's job: one family of strategies, one
                    strategy at a time.
  Shadow            The strategy runs and logs what it WOULD do, with no
                    orders. Ten clean days of that before it trades.
  Heat              How much of the whole council's money is at risk
                    right now across all open trades. Capped at 4%.
  PDT               A US rule: four day-trades in five days under $25k
                    locks the account. Applies to paper too. We do not
                    day-trade equities.
  IEX feed          The free price feed our paper account sees; close to
                    the real market, not identical. Disclosed.
  Money board       One line per business idea: real dollars in, real
                    dollars out, hours, and the next action. Zero until
                    a payment is confirmed.
