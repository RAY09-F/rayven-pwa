WHAT EXISTS

You are given a finished, working page: a single self-contained HTML file with no
libraries and no build step. It renders an armoured humanoid bust out of 64,477 points
in raw WebGL — the body is cut into 54 plates, each flattened onto its own plane, lifted
clear of the form and rimmed in light, with dark seams carved between them. One body
carries three personas. Switching persona does not rebuild the figure; the five palette
colours are shader uniforms, so it crossfades temperature over 900ms and changes tempo.

  THOR   blue / white,  gold rims        ground #04070F   tempo 1.00  steady
  LOKI   green / black, acid-lime rims   ground #030805   tempo 1.22  restless
  ODIN   white / gold,  cold-white rims  ground #0A0805   tempo 0.82  slow, heavy

It reacts to the microphone (visor bar, plate lift, rim burn, turbulence — fast attack,
slow release), to the pointer, and to four states: IDLE, LISTENING, THINKING, SPEAKING.

THE RULE THAT MATTERS MOST

Do not touch the particle engine. Not the geometry, not the plate sampling, not the
shaders, not the state machine, not the palettes. That figure is approved and final.
Everything you change lives in the CSS and the chrome markup — the type, the layout,
the readouts, the controls, the way information sits around the form.

If you touch the shader at all, know this: `out`, `in`, `sample`, `filter`, `input` and
`output` are reserved words in GLSL. One of them used as a variable name compiles to
nothing and the page renders black with no error. That has already happened once.

WHAT I WANT FROM YOU

Three complete design directions for the chrome. Three separate full HTML files, each
one the whole working page — never a snippet, never a diff, never "replace this block".
I cannot merge code by hand.

Each direction must be a genuinely different answer to the same question, not a restyle:

  01  INSTRUMENT — the page reads like a precision instrument the figure is mounted in.
      Dense, small, technical, tabular numbers, hairline rules, everything justified to
      an invisible grid. Cold and exact.

  02  MONUMENT — almost nothing on screen. Enormous confident type, vast negative space,
      one line of text and one way in. The figure carries the whole page.

  03  THRESHOLD — the chrome behaves like a door being opened. The interface is quiet at
      rest and assembles itself around the figure as you engage with it — on hover, on
      voice, on persona change. Motion is the design.

CONSTRAINTS, ALL OF THEM NON-NEGOTIABLE

- One file each. Self-contained. No React, no Tailwind, no bundler, no npm.
  A Google font link is allowed and must degrade to a real system fallback stack.
- No localStorage, no sessionStorage, no network calls, no third-party script.
- The bright shoulders of the figure fill the bottom third of the frame. Any text placed
  there needs its own scrim or it is unreadable. Check it, do not assume it.
- Works at 1600x900, 900x700 and 390x844. Nothing overflows sideways at any width.
  At 390 wide the primary action must still be reachable with a thumb.
- Honour `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Keep the whole public API working exactly as it is: `ASGARD.persona()`, `.state()`,
  `.level()`, `.auto()`, `.say()`, `.metric()`, `.onPersona`, `.onMic`, and the `DATA`
  object that every displayed value already comes from. The backend wires to those.
- Keep the 2D canvas fallback working for machines with no WebGL.
- No character likenesses, no logos, no brand marks, no trademarked wordmarks. The names
  Thor, Loki and Odin are the Norse figures, nothing else.

WHAT I DO NOT WANT

An orb in the middle of the screen with a chat panel down the right-hand side. I have
been shown that layout thirty-nine times and it is dead. Do not send it back to me again.
No stock dashboard cards. No glassmorphism. No emoji anywhere.

DELIVER

Three files. For each one, three sentences on what the idea is and what it costs.
Then tell me plainly which of the three you would actually ship, and why.

The HUD — the in-conversation interface inside each hall — is a separate job and is not
part of this brief. Design the door, not the room.
